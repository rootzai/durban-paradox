# Chapter 6: The Cannabis Consciousness

![A surreal landscape where the clouds are made of green smoke forming human faces, and the trees are giant neurons connecting to the sky, Renaissance painting style](/images/chapter6_cannabis_consciousness.png)

**The Indian Ocean, February 1498**

The Indian Ocean was not like the Atlantic. The Atlantic was a brute, a chaotic system of storms and fury, a barroom brawler who punched you in the face and then stole your wallet. The Indian Ocean was a seductress. It was warm, blue, and deceptively calm. It lulled you into a sense of security, then whispered secrets in your ear that made you question your sanity. It was a liquid mirror that reflected not your face, but your fears.

The crew was restless. Scurvy had begun to set in, turning strong men into gums-bleeding ghosts who looked like they had been chewing on red crayons. The water in the casks was foul, smelling of swamp gas and regret. The biscuits were more weevil than flour; the men had taken to tapping them on the table before eating, a grim percussion section for their misery.

But MPG was thriving. He had his pipe. He had his Mpombo stone. And he had the Cannabis Consciousness.

He had begun to experiment with the *dagga* he had traded for in Natal. He found that if he smoked it at specific times of the day—sunrise, high noon, sunset—it aligned his circadian rhythms with the rotation of the Earth. He felt... tuned. Like a lute string that had finally found its perfect tension.

"It is not a drug," he explained to Brother António, who was looking increasingly skeletal and was spending a lot of time praying to St. Elmo. "It is a lens. A cognitive prosthetic. It allows me to see the hidden variables. It allows me to perceive the non-linear aspects of reality."

"It smells like the devil's flatulence," Brother António muttered, crossing himself with a trembling hand. "And it makes you talk about 'fractals' and 'recursive loops.' These are not godly words."

"The devil is just a metaphor for entropy," MPG said, packing his pipe with the precision of a surgeon. "And this... this is negentropy. This is order out of chaos. This is the geometry of the divine."

He lit the pipe. The smoke curled up, forming shapes that looked suspiciously like Mobius strips.

"I have been thinking about the Paradox," MPG said, leaning back against the mast. "The Durban Paradox. Why is it that we seek the East by going West? Why do we seek wealth by risking everything? Why do we seek God by building towers of stone when he is clearly in the spaces between the stones?"

"Because we are sinners," Brother António said, closing his eyes.

"No," MPG said, shaking his head. "Because we are incomplete. We are trying to close the loop. We are trying to solve the equation of our own existence. But the equation has no solution. That is the joke. That is the cosmic punchline. We are an equation that equals... nothing. Or everything. Depending on how you define zero."

He took a deep drag. His eyes dilated. He saw the ship not as wood and canvas, but as a vector in a multi-dimensional vector space. He saw the crew not as men, but as biological machines, processing energy and information. He saw the wind not as air, but as a flow of probability.

"We are not exploring the world," MPG whispered, his voice vibrating with awe. "We are exploring ourselves. The map is a mirror. And right now, the mirror is very, very foggy."

He grabbed his lute and began to play. But this time, he didn't play music. He played math. He played the Fibonacci sequence. *Pluck. Pluck. Pluck-pluck. Pluck-pluck-pluck.* He played the prime numbers. He played the square root of two, which was very dissonant and made the ship's dog howl.

The crew gathered around. They didn't understand the math, but they felt the vibration. They felt the order. For a moment, the scurvy didn't matter. The thirst didn't matter. They were part of a great, cosmic symphony. They were variables in a grand equation.

"He is a wizard," Gaspar whispered, his eyes wide. "He is summoning the spirits of the deep."

"No," da Gama said, watching from the quarterdeck, his face unreadable. "He is a navigator. But he is not navigating the sea. He is navigating the mind. And I fear he has lost the chart."

![A field of cannabis plants, but the leaves are fractal patterns that repeat infinitely. The sky above is a swirling vortex of green and gold. Surreal, psychedelic art style.](/images/chapter6_cannabis_fractal.png)

That night, MPG had a dream. He dreamt he was a point on a line. He was moving forward, always forward. But the line was curving. It was bending back on itself. He was approaching his starting point. But he was higher up. It was a spiral. A helix.

He woke up with a start. The cabin was dark, smelling of salt and old socks.

"The circle!" he shouted, sitting up. "It's a circle! Time is a circle! But it's a spiral! We are moving up!"

He ran to da Gama's cabin, wearing only his nightshirt and his purple spectacles. The captain was asleep, his sword by his side, dreaming of spices.

"Vasco!" MPG shouted, shaking him. "Wake up! I have solved it! I have solved the geometry of history!"

"What?" da Gama groaned, reaching for his sword. "Are we under attack? Is it the Moors?"

"No!" MPG cried, his eyes wild. "We are under an illusion! We think we are moving forward in time! But we are not! We are moving in a loop! The past is the future! The future is the past! We are sailing into our own history! We are the ancestors of our descendants!"

Da Gama looked at him. He looked at the purple glasses. He looked at the wild hair. He smelled the lingering scent of *dagga*.

"Go to sleep, Glassworthy. You are high. You are higher than the mainmast."

"I am not high!" MPG insisted, waving his hands. "I am elevated! I am at a higher orbital shell of consciousness! Don't you see? The reason we can't find India is because we are looking for it in space! But India is in time! We have to sail *when*! We have to adjust our temporal coordinates!"

Da Gama sighed. He pointed to the door. "Get out. Before I throw you in the brig. Or the ocean. Whichever is closer."

MPG left the cabin. He went back to the deck. He looked at the stars. They were different now. They were not just lights. They were coordinates. They were a clock face.

"I will show them," he whispered to the universe. "I will show them all. I will sail this ship into tomorrow. Or yesterday. Whichever comes first."

He took another puff of his pipe. The smoke drifted up, forming a perfect question mark against the velvet night.

The Cannabis Consciousness had opened the door. Now, MPG just had to walk through it. And hope there wasn't a sheer drop on the other side. Or a dragon. Dragons were notoriously bad at math.

**The Recursive Dream**

In his altered state, MPG began to perceive the ship's journey as a fractal. Every day was a smaller version of the whole voyage. Every wave was a smaller version of the ocean. Every meal of weevil-biscuits was a smaller version of the famine of the soul.

"We are living in a self-similar universe," he told the ship's cat, a mangy creature named Schrödinger (a name MPG had chosen because the cat was always half-asleep and half-awake, and thus existed in a quantum superposition of states). "If we look closely enough at a grain of sand, we will see the entire coastline of Africa. If we look closely enough at a moment, we will see eternity."

The cat meowed, which MPG interpreted as a confirmation of the Holographic Principle.

"Exactly," MPG nodded. "The whole is contained in the part. Which means... if I can solve the equation of this cat, I can solve the equation of the universe."

He stared at the cat. The cat stared back.

"Meow," the cat said.

"42," MPG whispered. "Of course. It was so simple."
