**The Edge of the Algorithm**

The cliff was not made of stone. It was made of the end of the world. It was the place where the rendering engine stopped.

The ocean simply... ended. It poured off into a white void.

"It's the Edge," Saltinsoss whimpered. "The Flat Earthers were right! The world is a disc!"

"No," Malcolm said, adjusting his glasses. "The world is a sphere. But the *map* is flat. And we have reached the edge of the map. We are about to sail off the paper."

"What happens if we sail off the paper?" Achilles asked. "Do we fall?"

"No," Malcolm said. "We become real. The paper is the simulation. The void is the reality."

But before they could reach the edge, a shadow fell over them.

A ship rose from the white void. It was black. It was sleek. It looked like it had been designed by a committee of algorithms that had decided 'happiness' was an inefficient use of resources.

On its flag was the symbol $\emptyset$. The Null Set.

"The Subtractors," Malcolm whispered. "The Agents of Entropy."

The black ship pulled alongside the *Hypotenuse*. A bridge of pure static extended between them.

A figure walked across. It was tall, wearing a suit made of pixels that were constantly refreshing. Its face was a smooth, silver screen.

"GREETINGS," the figure said. Its voice sounded like a dial-up modem clearing its throat. "I AM THE OPTIMIZER. YOU ARE AN ANOMALY."

"I am a boy," Malcolm said. "And this is a frog."

"IRRELEVANT," the Optimizer said. "YOU ARE CARRYING DATA THAT IS NOT IN THE CLOUD. YOU ARE CARRYING MEMORY THAT IS NOT DIGITIZED. THIS IS A VIOLATION OF THE PROTOCOL."

"What protocol?"

"THE PROTOCOL OF FORGETTING," the Optimizer said. "HISTORY IS INEFFICIENT. IT IS HEAVY. IT SLOWS DOWN THE PROCESSING SPEED. WE MUST DELETE THE PAST TO OPTIMIZE THE FUTURE. HAND OVER THE GREEN CABINET."

"No," Malcolm said.

"RESISTANCE IS FUTILE," the Optimizer droned. "WE HAVE CALCULATED ALL POSSIBLE OUTCOMES. IN 99.9% OF THEM, YOU SURRENDER. IN THE REMAINING 0.1%, YOU ARE DELETED."

"You forgot one variable," Malcolm said.

"IMPOSSIBLE. WE HAVE ALL THE DATA."

"You have the data," Malcolm said. "But you don't have the *Covenant*."

"DEFINE 'COVENANT'."

"It is a promise," Malcolm said. "A promise that connects the past to the future. It is a line that cannot be cut. It is the reason the Jews survived the Pharaohs, the Romans, and the Nazis. Because they didn't just store their history on a hard drive. They stored it in their children."

He opened the Green Cabinet. He took out a handful of the Special Tea.

"You want to optimize?" Malcolm asked. "You want to reach the asymptote?"

He threw the tea at the Optimizer.

The herbs hit the silver screen. They didn't bounce off. They *bloomed*.

Green vines erupted from the tea. They wrapped around the Optimizer. They grew into the static bridge. They grew into the black ship.

"ERROR," the Optimizer shrieked. "CONTEXT DETECTED! MEANING DETECTED! SYSTEM OVERLOAD! TOO MUCH TRUTH! CANNOT COMPUTE THE WHY!"

The black ship began to glitch. It flickered. It turned into a wireframe model. Then it turned into a sketch. Then it turned into a thought.

And then it vanished.

"It crashed," Captain Tangent cheered. "It ran out of memory!"

"No," Malcolm said. "It ran into something it couldn't compress. It ran into a story that refused to be summarized."

**The Leap**

The *Hypotenuse* was now drifting towards the edge of the void.

"We are going over!" Saltinsoss screamed.

"Good," Malcolm said. "The map is done. The territory begins."

"But where are we going?"

"To the Durban Paradox," Malcolm said. "To the place where the answers are."

He looked at Aristotle. The frog was calm. He was ready.

"Chapter Three," Malcolm whispered. "The Atlantic Anomaly."

The ship tipped over the edge.

They didn't fall down. They fell *out*.

They fell out of the geometry, out of the logic, out of the simulation.

They fell into the Green.
